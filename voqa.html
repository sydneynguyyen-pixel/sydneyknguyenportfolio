Context

Most of what local museums put on never reaches the people who'd want to go.

Museums and community spaces in San José host exhibits, talks and events all year. Most people only find out about them from a flyer, a busy events page, or not at all. Museum staff want to reach more people, especially younger ones, but they have little time and few channels beyond the ones they already use.

To understand the gap, we talked to people on both sides of it.

This was a 10-week team project in our graduate program at San José State University. Basmah, Chi and I spoke with museum staff and with people who visit museums, across four museums in San José.

Research

How we did the research.

We visited two museums in person and interviewed staff and visitors there. To hear from more museums, we also spoke with staff at the Rosicrucian Egyptian Museum and The Tech Interactive, and with independent curator Mara Holt Skov.

1

Observations

We visited the San Jose Museum of Art and the Children's Discovery Museum and watched how visitors moved through each space.

2

Interviews

We interviewed 10 museum staff and 8 visitors, in person at the museums and over Zoom.

3

Synthesis

We grouped what we heard into what each side wants and what gets in their way, and wrote two personas from it.

4

Prototype

We designed the screens in Figma and linked them into a clickable prototype.

The questions we asked.

We wrote one set of questions for museum staff and one for visitors.

Museum staff

  • How do museums define civic engagement?
  • How do they curate experiences?
  • What barriers do they face?
  • How do staff understand and practice their civic role?

Visitors

  • What motivates them to visit?
  • Do they feel represented?
  • What makes a museum memorable or relevant?
  • What makes deeper engagement difficult?

What museum staff told us.

Staff want visitors to think for themselves and to feel the museum is for them. Several told us their work behind the scenes rarely reaches the public.

What visitors told us.

Visitors want to learn at their own pace and to see people like them in what a museum shows. Julio told us a museum visit once led him to look up organizations and donate.

Personas

Two people we designed for.

We wrote one persona for the people who visit museums and one for the people who work in them.

The Visitor

A thoughtful museum-goer who learns through stories, conversation, and slow, meaningful engagement.

Goals
  • Discover new ideas, cultures and histories
  • Share experiences with family and friends
  • Walk away with a new perspective
Motivations
  • Emotional or inspiring experiences
  • Hands-on or participatory exhibits
  • Connecting themselves with art and culture
Frustrations
  • Dense text or unclear context
  • Exhibits that feel intimidating
  • Few chances to take part

The Professional

A mission-oriented professional shaping museums as spaces where all visitors feel represented and welcomed.

Goals
  • Design inclusive learning experiences
  • Highlight underrepresented stories
  • Build trust by working with the community
Motivations
  • Civic learning and social impact
  • Meeting different languages, sensory needs and ages
  • Helping visitors turn what they learn into action
Frustrations
  • Little visibility and marketing reach
  • Feedback that's sparse or one-sided
  • Visitors don't see the work behind the scenes

Define

One question for both sides.

Visitors wanted to find things that matter to them, and museums wanted to reach those visitors. We wrote one question that covers both.

Building it

We mapped the app first, then went straight to high-fidelity screens.

We mapped how someone moves through the app: signing in, finding museums on the home feed, opening a museum's page, and saving what they like to their profile. The map showed that finding things belongs on the home screen, not in a separate tab. We didn't make wireframes. We went straight to designing the screens in Figma.

The final screens.

Eighteen screens, from signing in to a saved collection.

Final Product

One app for finding museums, and for museums to find visitors.

VOQA shows people exhibits and events that match their interests, and gives museums one shared place to post what's on.

Reflection

What I learned, and what's next.

Finding museums was about relevance, not search.

The people we talked to didn't need a better search box. They needed to see things that matched what they already cared about. Once the home feed did that, the community feed, museum pages and collections all built on it.

Designing for two audiences at once.

It would have been easier to design only for visitors or only for museums. Visitors needed a feed worth checking, and museums needed pages that are quick to keep up to date, so each screen had to work for both.

What's next.

Test the feed with visitors and museum staff · make it easy for museums to update their pages · add notifications for saved interests.
